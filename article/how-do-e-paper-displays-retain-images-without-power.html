How Do E-Paper Displays Retain Images Without Power?

This article explores the science behind electronic paper (e-paper) displays and how they maintain static text and images without using electricity. By utilizing bistable electrophoretic microcapsules filled with charged pigment particles and a viscous suspending fluid, e-readers only consume power during page refreshes. The following sections break down the mechanics of electrophoresis, the structure of the microcapsules, and the physical forces that lock the pigments in place to achieve zero power consumption once an image is set.

The Structure of Electrophoretic Microcapsules

At the heart of an e-paper display is a film containing millions of tiny spheres called microcapsules, each roughly the diameter of a human hair. Inside every microcapsule is a clear, viscous fluid containing positively charged white pigment particles (typically titanium dioxide) and negatively charged black pigment particles.

These microcapsules are sandwiched between two layers of electrodes: a transparent top electrode layer and a bottom layer made of a grid of pixel electrodes.

Moving Pigments via Electrophoresis

To form an image or text on the screen, the e-reader applies a brief electrical charge to specific pixel electrodes. This process relies on electrophoresis, which is the movement of charged particles through a fluid under the influence of an electric field.

  • Creating Black Pixels: When a positive charge is applied to the bottom electrode, the positively charged white particles are repelled toward the bottom, while the negatively charged black particles are pulled to the top of the microcapsule. The viewer sees a black dot.
  • Creating White Pixels: When a negative charge is applied to the bottom electrode, the negatively charged black particles are pushed to the bottom, and the positively charged white particles rise to the top, creating a white surface.
  • Creating Grayscale: By varying the duration and strength of the voltage pulses, the electronics can mix the black and white particles at the top surface to create different shades of gray.

Achieving Bistability and Zero Power Consumption

Once the particles reach their designated positions at the top or bottom of the microcapsule, the electrical current is turned off completely. The ability of the display to hold this state without power is known as "bistability." E-paper achieves bistability through three primary physical mechanisms:

1. High Fluid Viscosity

The clear liquid inside the microcapsule is not water; it is a specialized hydrocarbon oil engineered to have a specific viscosity. Once the electric field is removed, the thickness of this fluid provides high hydrodynamic resistance, preventing the particles from quickly drifting out of place.

2. Density Matching

Gravity would normally cause the pigment particles to sink to the bottom of the capsule over time. To prevent this, manufacturers match the specific gravity (density) of the suspending fluid to the density of the coated pigment particles. Because the particles are neutrally buoyant, they remain suspended exactly where the electric field leaves them.

3. Van der Waals and Electrostatic Forces

When the pigment particles are driven to the inner walls of the microcapsule, weak intermolecular attractions known as Van der Waals forces, along with residual electrostatic charges on the capsule walls, cause the particles to lightly adhere to the surface and to each other. These forces are strong enough to keep the particles locked in position against normal movement and vibration, yet weak enough to be easily overcome the next time a voltage is applied for a page refresh.

Because of these combined factors, an e-paper screen requires zero power to maintain a page of text for days, weeks, or even months, only drawing battery power for the milliseconds required to rearrange the pigments when you turn the page.
