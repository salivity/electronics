How Thermal Relief Pads Prevent Cold Solder Joints

This article explains the critical role of spoke-shaped thermal relief pads in printed circuit board (PCB) design and manufacturing. It covers why large copper pours act as unwanted heat sinks during the soldering process, how this rapid heat dissipation leads to defective cold solder joints, and how the specific geometry of thermal relief spokes restricts heat flow to ensure a reliable electrical and mechanical bond without sacrificing current capacity.

The Problem with Large Copper Pours

In PCB design, large areas of copper—often used for ground or power planes—are essential for reducing electrical noise, handling high currents, and dissipating heat generated by active components during normal operation. However, copper's exceptional thermal conductivity becomes a major obstacle during the assembly and soldering process.

To create a successful solder joint, both the component lead and the copper pad on the PCB must be heated simultaneously to a temperature above the melting point of the solder. When a component pad is connected directly and completely to a massive copper pour, the pour acts as a giant heat sink. As soon as a soldering iron or reflow oven applies heat to the pad, the surrounding copper rapidly draws that thermal energy away from the joint area.

How Heat Sinking Causes Cold Solder Joints

Because the large copper plane wicks heat away faster than the soldering equipment can supply it, the component pad fails to reach the optimal wetting temperature. When molten solder is applied to a pad that is too cool, the solder cannot properly bond with the copper surface.

This results in a defect known as a cold solder joint. Cold joints are characterized by a dull, grainy, or beaded appearance rather than a smooth, shiny concave fillet. They form weak mechanical connections that are prone to cracking under physical stress or thermal expansion. Electrically, they create high-resistance points or intermittent connections that can cause the entire electronic device to malfunction or fail completely over time.

The Mechanics of Spoke-Shaped Thermal Relief Pads

To solve the heat-sinking problem without disconnecting the pad from the copper pour, PCB designers use spoke-shaped thermal relief pads (often called "thermals"). Instead of the copper plane flooding entirely over the pad, a ring-shaped gap is etched around the pad, leaving only two to four narrow copper bridges, or "spokes," connecting the pad to the surrounding plane.

These spokes function as thermal bottlenecks. By drastically reducing the cross-sectional area of the copper connecting the pad to the plane, the spokes increase the thermal resistance between the two areas.

When heat is applied to a thermal relief pad during soldering:

  1. The thermal energy flows into the pad.
  2. The narrow spokes restrict the speed at which the heat can escape into the surrounding copper pour.
  3. Heat concentrates directly at the pad and component lead, allowing them to quickly reach the temperature required for solder to flow and wet properly.
  4. Once the heat source is removed, the joint cools at a normal rate, forming a strong, reliable intermetallic bond.

Balancing Electrical and Thermal Needs

While the spokes are narrow enough to restrict rapid heat transfer during the brief window of the soldering process, they are wide enough to maintain a solid electrical connection. For standard signal pins and low-to-medium current ground connections, two to four spokes easily handle the required electrical load without introducing significant resistance or inductance.

During the automated reflow soldering of surface-mount components, thermal reliefs also prevent an issue called "tombstoning." If one pad of a two-pin component (like a resistor) is connected to a large copper pour and the other is connected to a thin trace, the solder on the thin trace will melt first. The surface tension of the melting solder pulls the component upright before the other pad gets hot enough to melt its solder. Adding thermal relief spokes equalizes the heating rate of both pads, keeping the component flat and properly aligned.
