IPC-A-610 Class 1 vs Class 3 Solder Fillet and Barrel Fill

This article provides a direct comparison between IPC-A-610 Class 1 and Class 3 electronics assemblies, specifically focusing on the requirements for solder fillet wetting angles and plated through-hole (PTH) barrel fill percentages. Understanding these specific geometric and volumetric thresholds is essential for manufacturers and inspectors determining whether a printed circuit board assembly meets the baseline criteria for general consumer goods or the stringent demands of high-reliability, mission-critical applications.

Defining IPC-A-610 Classes

Before analyzing the specific solder metrics, it is necessary to define the operational environments of the two classes:

  • Class 1 (General Electronic Products): Includes consumer products suitable for applications where the major requirement is the function of the completed assembly. Cosmetics and extreme longevity are secondary.
  • Class 3 (High Performance/Harsh Environment Electronic Products): Includes equipment where continued high performance or performance-on-demand is critical. Equipment downtime cannot be tolerated, end-use environments may be uncommonly harsh, and the equipment must function when required, such as in life support, aerospace, or military systems.

Plated Through-Hole (PTH) Barrel Fill Percentages

Vertical fill (barrel fill) measures how much of the plated through-hole cylinder is filled with solder during wave, selective, or hand soldering processes. This metric is a primary separator between Class 1 and Class 3 reliability.

Standard Vertical Fill

  • Class 1 Requirement: Requires a minimum of 50% vertical barrel fill. Up to 50% of the barrel can remain void of solder, provided the lead and land show evidence of wetting on the solder destination side or source side as required.
  • Class 3 Requirement: Requires a minimum of 75% vertical barrel fill. A maximum of 25% of the barrel height can remain unfilled.

Thermal Relief and Heavy Ground Planes

When a through-hole component lead is connected to a heat-sinking layer (like an internal ground or power plane) without thermal relief, achieving full barrel fill is more difficult. IPC-A-610 adjusts the requirements for these specific instances:

  • Class 1 Requirement: Minimum 50% vertical fill remains the standard.
  • Class 3 Requirement: The requirement is relaxed from 75% down to a minimum of 50% vertical fill, provided there is 360-degree wetting on the solder source side and the lead is wetted.

Circumferential Wetting

Alongside vertical fill, circumferential wetting (the coverage around the lead and barrel wall) also separates the classes:

  • Class 1: Requires a minimum of 180 degrees of circumferential wetting and fillet formation on the solder source side.
  • Class 3: Requires a minimum of 270 degrees of circumferential wetting on the primary side (destination side) and 330 degrees on the secondary side (solder source side).

Solder Fillet Wetting Angles

The wetting angle (or contact angle) is the angle formed between the solder fillet and the termination pad or component lead. It indicates the metallurgical bond quality. A smaller angle indicates better wetting and flow, while a larger angle (>90 degrees) indicates poor wetting, cold solder, or contamination.

The 90-Degree Threshold

Unlike barrel fill, IPC-A-610 does not assign different numerical degree limits to Class 1 versus Class 3 for the wetting angle itself. Instead, the standard uses a universal baseline for acceptable wetting across all classes:

  • Target Condition: The ideal solder fillet appears smooth and concave, tapering seamlessly to the copper land and component lead. This typically represents a wetting angle between 15 degrees and 45 degrees.
  • Acceptable Condition (Class 1, 2, and 3): The wetting angle must not exceed 90 degrees. A convex fillet is acceptable only if the solder is caused by excess solder volume, does not obscure the lead contour (where required), and the wetting angle at the very edge of the solder toe remains less than 90 degrees.
  • Defect Condition (Class 1, 2, and 3): A wetting angle that exceeds 90 degrees ( creating a "balled" appearance at the interface) indicates non-wetting or dewetting. This is a defect in both Class 1 and Class 3, EXCEPT when the contour of the solder extends over the edge of the land (solder resist interface), where a >90-degree angle is naturally formed by the containment of the pad boundary.

How Fillet Geometry Separates Class 1 and Class 3

While the 90-degree maximum wetting angle applies to both classes, the extent of the fillet (heel and toe heights) where this wetting angle must be maintained differs drastically for Surface Mount Technology (SMT):

  • Gull Wing Leads (Minimum Heel Fillet Height):
    • Class 1: Requires a wetted fillet, which may not extend very high up the lead.
    • Class 3: The solder fillet must extend up the heel of the lead to a minimum height equal to the solder thickness plus 100% of the lead thickness (G + T), or up to the knee of the lead, whichever is less.
  • Rectangular/Chip Components (End Joint Width and Height):
    • Class 1: Requires a visibly wetted fillet; minimum end overlap is just 50% of the component termination width.
    • Class 3: Requires a minimum end joint width of 75% of the component termination. Furthermore, the fillet height must reach at least the solder thickness plus 25% of the component height (G + 25% H) or 0.5 mm, whichever is less.

Summary of Key Separators

To quickly differentiate a Class 1 assembly from a Class 3 assembly during inspection:

  1. PTH Barrel Fill: Class 1 passes at 50% fill; Class 3 requires 75% fill.
  2. Wetting Angles: Both classes fail if the wetting angle exceeds 90 degrees due to poor wetting. Both classes require concave fillets as the baseline.
  3. Fillet Volume/Height: Class 3 requires the <90-degree wetted fillet to climb significantly higher up the component lead or termination (up to 100% of lead thickness on gull wings) compared to Class 1, which only requires the presence of a properly wetted joint at the base.
