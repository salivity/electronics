Why Nanosheet Gate-All-Around FETs Are Replacing FinFETs at 2nm

The semiconductor industry is making a monumental shift from FinFET (Fin Field-Effect Transistor) technology to Nanosheet Gate-All-Around (GAA) FETs as it transitions to the 2-nanometer (2nm) manufacturing node. This article provides a comprehensive overview of why this transition is necessary for advanced electronics. At the 2nm scale, traditional FinFETs suffer from severe physical limitations, particularly current leakage and degraded electrostatic control. Nanosheet GAAFETs solve these problems by completely surrounding the conductive channel with the gate, allowing for superior power efficiency, variable transistor sizing, and enhanced performance.

The Physical Limits of FinFET at the 2nm Node

FinFETs have been the backbone of advanced chip manufacturing since their introduction around the 22nm node. In a FinFET, the conductive channel rises vertically like a fin, and the gate wraps around it on three sides (left, right, and top). This three-sided grip gave engineers enough control over the electrical current to shrink transistors down to the 3nm node.

However, shrinking transistors to the 2nm scale brings devastating short-channel effects. As the distance between the source and the drain becomes microscopically small, the gate loses its ability to completely stop the flow of electrons through the bottom of the fin, which remains unenclosed. This results in subthreshold leakage—electrons flowing when the transistor is supposed to be turned off. This leakage wastes massive amounts of power and generates excess heat, making FinFETs unviable at 2nm.

Superior Electrostatic Control with Gate-All-Around

Nanosheet GAAFETs overcome the FinFET's limitations by altering the physical geometry of the transistor. Instead of vertical fins, GAAFETs use horizontal sheets of silicon stacked vertically on top of one another. The gate material is then deposited so that it completely surrounds each individual nanosheet on all four sides.

By wrapping the gate entirely around the channel, engineers achieve maximum electrostatic control. There is no unenclosed bottom path for electrons to leak through. This complete encapsulation drastically reduces current leakage when the transistor is in its "off" state, directly translating to much lower power consumption for the overall chip.

Overcoming Fin Quantization with Variable Nanosheet Widths

Another critical reason for replacing FinFETs is a design limitation known as fin quantization. In a FinFET, the fins must be manufactured at fixed thicknesses and heights. If a chip designer needs a transistor to carry more current, they cannot simply make a wider fin; they have to add a second or third fin. This rigid structure wastes valuable silicon real estate and limits design flexibility.

Nanosheet GAAFETs eliminate this issue. Because the silicon sheets are oriented horizontally, chip designers can easily adjust the width of the nanosheets during the manufacturing process without increasing the overall footprint of the transistor.

  • Wider nanosheets allow more electrical current to flow, maximizing drive strength for high-performance computing tasks.
  • Narrower nanosheets restrict the current and minimize capacitance, optimizing the transistor for ultra-low-power efficiency.

Higher Drive Current and Density

At the 2nm node, electronics require more performance packed into a smaller area. Stacking multiple nanosheets vertically allows a GAAFET to achieve a higher effective channel width within the same horizontal footprint as a FinFET.

Because the gate touches a larger surface area of the conductive channel in this stacked configuration, the transistor can drive significantly more electrical current at lower operating voltages. This means 2nm chips utilizing Nanosheet GAAFETs can operate at higher clock speeds while simultaneously drawing less battery power, paving the way for the next generation of artificial intelligence processors, mobile devices, and high-performance servers.
